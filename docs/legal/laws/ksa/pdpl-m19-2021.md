## Summary (≤ 200 words)

The PDPL is KSA's first comprehensive horizontal data protection statute. It applies to any processing of personal data of individuals residing in KSA, by controllers inside or outside the Kingdom. It defines personal data broadly, recognises sensitive data (health, biometric, genetic, financial, religious belief), and establishes data subject rights (access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent). Controllers must register sensitive processing activities (originally a National Data Bank registration requirement — softened in the 2024 Executive Regulations). Cross-border transfer is restricted: a 2024 amendment shifted from strict prohibition to a risk-based regime (adequacy decisions by SDAIA, binding instruments, derogations). The law requires consent as a default lawful basis with narrow exceptions (legitimate interest of the controller, vital interest, public interest, legal obligation). Breach notification to SDAIA is required within 72 hours of awareness when there is risk to data subjects. Penalties include fines up to SAR 5 million and criminal liability (imprisonment up to 2 years) for unlawful disclosure of sensitive data.

### Article 5 — Lawful basis for processing

**Text (Arabic):**

**Text (English summary):**

> Processing requires consent of the data subject unless one of the limited exceptions applies: (a) processing serves the actual interest of the data subject and contacting them is impossible; (b) processing is required by law; (c) processing is required for a public-interest purpose; (d) processing is required by the controller for a legitimate interest that does not prejudice the data subject's rights.

**Aooda impact:** Aooda's merchant clients are the controllers; Aooda is processor. The merchant must hold a valid consent (or fall under an exception) before Aooda ingests, segments, or messages the data subject. Aooda must contractually require the merchant to warrant lawful basis.

### Article 35 — Penalties

**Text (English summary):**

> Disclosure or publication of sensitive personal data with intent to harm or for personal benefit: up to 2 years imprisonment + fine up to SAR 3 million. Other violations: administrative fines up to SAR 5 million, doubled for repeat offences. Warning, suspension of processing, or revocation of licence may also be imposed.

**Aooda impact:** Material exposure. Sensitive data (financial, health) appearing in customer support transcripts triggers the criminal tier.

## Penalties

- Criminal: imprisonment up to 2 years + fine up to SAR 3M for unlawful disclosure of sensitive data with intent to harm.
- Administrative: fines up to SAR 5M, doubled for repeat offences; warnings; suspension of processing; revocation of registration.
- Civil: compensation to data subjects for proven harm.
