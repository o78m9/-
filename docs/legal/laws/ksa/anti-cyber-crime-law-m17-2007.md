## Summary (≤ 200 words)

The Anti-Cyber Crime Law criminalises unauthorised access to information systems, interception of data in transit, system damage, identity-related fraud via electronic means, and content offences (defamation, privacy invasion via electronic publication). It predates the PDPL and remains in force alongside it. Penalties range from fines to imprisonment, with aggravating tiers for accessing private/personal data, financial systems, and critical infrastructure.

## Articles relevant to Aooda

### Article 3 — Unauthorised access + privacy interception

**Text (English summary):**

> Imprisonment up to 1 year + fine up to SAR 500,000 for: (a) unauthorised access to a website/system/computer/network with intent to threaten/blackmail; (b) intercepting data in transit without authorisation; (c) invasion of privacy through misuse of mobile camera or similar device.

**Aooda impact:** Aooda's engineers and contractors who access merchant customer data must have explicit authorisation logged. RBAC must be enforced.

**Compliance gap:** No RBAC audit log; no contractor access controls documented.

### Article 5 — Aggravated breach (access to private/personal data)

**Text (English summary):**

> Imprisonment up to 4 years + fine up to SAR 3M for unauthorised access resulting in disclosure of data, modification, or destruction; higher tier for sensitive/personal data.

**Aooda impact:** A rogue engineer dumping merchant customer DB = direct criminal exposure for Aooda employees and potentially officers.

## Penalties

Up to 10 years imprisonment + SAR 5M fine (for terrorist-financing aggravated offence); typical tier 1-4 years + SAR 500k-3M.
