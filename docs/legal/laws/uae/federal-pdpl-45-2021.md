## Summary (≤ 200 words)

The UAE Federal PDPL is the first horizontal data protection law applicable to the UAE mainland (DIFC and ADGM have their own regimes). It applies extraterritorially to controllers/processors targeting UAE data subjects. It defines personal data, sensitive data (health, biometric, genetic, racial, religious, political, criminal), and establishes data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, withdraw consent). Cross-border transfer requires either adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards (BCRs, contracts), or specific derogations. The law uses GDPR-style lawful bases (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public interest, legitimate interest — though the latter is narrowly framed). Breach notification timing is to be defined by Executive Regulations (still pending). Data Protection Officer is required for: large-scale sensitive processing, systematic monitoring, or high-risk profiling. Executive Regulations have not been issued as of the cutoff — the law is partially operational.

## Articles relevant to Aooda

### Article 5 — Lawful bases

**Text (English summary):**

> Processing requires one of: (a) consent, (b) necessity for contract performance, (c) legal obligation, (d) protection of public interest, (e) protection of data subject's interest, (f) compelling legitimate interest of controller (where rights of data subject do not prevail), (g) processing required for judicial/security purposes.

**Aooda impact:** Aooda's merchant typically relies on (a) or (b). Aooda must contractually require the merchant to declare basis at onboarding.

### Article 13 — Right to object to direct marketing

**Text (English summary):**

> Data subject has an absolute right to object to processing for direct marketing purposes, including profiling related to direct marketing, at any time and free of charge.

**Aooda impact:** Opt-out is non-negotiable. Aooda must honor immediately and across channels.

**Compliance gap:** Cross-channel opt-out propagation not yet built.

### Article 22 — Cross-border transfer

**Text (English summary):**

> Transfer permitted to jurisdictions with adequate protection (per UAE Data Office assessment), or under appropriate safeguards (binding rules, contracts), or derogations (consent, contract necessity, public interest, legal claim, vital interest).

**Aooda impact:** Same as KSA — every external subprocessor + every cross-border data flow needs a mechanism.

### Article 26 — Penalties

**Text (English summary):**

> Administrative penalties to be specified by Executive Regulations / Cabinet Decision. Currently undetermined in public form.
