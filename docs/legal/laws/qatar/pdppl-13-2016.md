## Summary (≤ 200 words)

Qatar PDPPL 13/2016 was the GCC's first horizontal data protection law. It applies to processing of personal data in Qatar (and arguably extraterritorially where Qatari data subjects are targeted). It defines personal data, sensitive data (health, race, ethnicity, children's data, religion, criminal record, marital status), and provides data subject rights (access, correction, deletion, restriction). Consent is the primary lawful basis with limited exceptions. Direct marketing requires explicit consent and an opt-out mechanism. Cross-border transfer is not heavily restricted compared to KSA/UAE but the recipient must offer comparable protection. NCSA (formerly the Ministry of Transport and Communications) is the regulator. Penalties include fines up to QAR 5M.

## Articles relevant to Aooda

### Article 4 — Lawful processing

> Personal data may be processed only after the individual's consent or where processing is necessary for a lawful purpose recognised in the law.

### Article 17 — Direct marketing

> Explicit consent required for direct marketing communications. Each communication must enable opt-out.

**Aooda impact:** Marketing without opt-in = direct violation. Opt-out compulsory in every message.

### Article 18 — Children's data

Special protection for children's data; parental consent required.

### Article 20 — Breach notification

Notify NCSA + data subject of breaches.

### Article 21 — Penalties

Fines QAR 1M (first tier) to QAR 5M (sensitive data tier).

## Penalties

QAR 1M – QAR 5M administrative fines; doubling for repeat; activity restriction orders.
