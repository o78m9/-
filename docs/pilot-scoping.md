# Pilot Scoping — First Named Clinic

> Owner: Customer Success
> Status: Draft for founder review
> Goal: Close ONE Saudi dental clinic on a named, paid 30-day pilot whose results we can publish on the landing page.
> Tone for this doc: operator, not consultant. If a section doesn't move us toward a signed clinic and a publishable case study, it's cut.

---

## 1. Pilot definition

**Duration:** 30 calendar days, starting Day 0 (kickoff call).

**Pricing for this pilot (recommended):**

- **Hybrid: free trial + earned-revenue share, capped.**
- Clinic pays **0 SAR upfront**.
- Clinic pays **15% of recovered revenue** (not the standard 20%) — only on visits that happen _and_ get paid inside the 30-day window AND are attributable via the unique WhatsApp link / clinic-side confirmation.
- Hard cap: clinic will never pay more than **3,000 SAR** during the pilot, regardless of recovered revenue.
- If zero patients return in 30 days → **clinic pays zero**, we walk away, no hard feelings.
- After the pilot, if they continue, price snaps back to the standard 20% with no cap.

**Why this structure (not standard 20%, not fully free):**

- Fully free attracts tire-kickers and gives us no signal on willingness to pay.
- Standard 20% with no cap scares a conservative owner who hasn't seen the product work yet — Abu-Khalid will not sign an open-ended revenue share with a stranger.
- 15% with a cap respects the published price page (it's a _pilot discount on the same model_, not a different model) and gives the clinic upside: "if it works gangbusters, you pay less than the public price; if it flops, you pay nothing."
- Cap of 3,000 SAR ≈ the cost of a part-time receptionist for ~2 weeks. That's the mental anchor Abu-Khalid uses.

**Scope — what's IN the pilot:**

- One clinic location, one WhatsApp number.
- One patient cohort: **dormant patients who last visited 6–18 months ago** (not too cold, not too recent). Cap the cohort at **300 patients** for round one to keep quality high.
- Message types: (a) reactivation outreach, (b) one follow-up if no reply after 5 days, (c) booking confirmation handoff back to the clinic's receptionist. No promotional blasts, no marketing copy.
- Opt-out flow: every message ends with "للإيقاف رد بـ (إيقاف)" — a single Arabic word handles the unsubscribe. Opt-outs are hard-deleted from the queue within 60 minutes and flagged in the clinic's dashboard.
- All outbound copy reviewed by the clinic owner (or their designate) BEFORE the first send wave. Owner has final veto on tone.

**Scope — what's OUT of the pilot:**

- No new-patient acquisition messaging.
- No promotional discounts or offers in the messages (we test the relationship, not the discount).
- No multi-branch.
- No integration with the clinic's existing PMS — CSV import only.
- No analytics dashboard rebuild; we deliver weekly results in a 1-page WhatsApp PDF.

**Exit criteria for the clinic (they can leave clean):**

- Any time in first 7 days: full exit, no fee, we delete their data within 48 hours and send written confirmation.
- After Day 7: they can stop sending at any time, no future charges; only pay 15% on patients who already returned and paid.
- Data export: full CSV of all messages sent + opt-outs + booking outcomes, delivered within 5 business days of exit request.

**Exit criteria for Aooda (we can walk):**

- Clinic refuses to provide a clean patient list within 7 days of Day 0.
- Clinic insists on message copy that violates WhatsApp Business policy or Saudi consumer protection norms.
- Clinic uses the WhatsApp number for spam unrelated to the pilot during the 30 days.
- Owner becomes unreachable for > 7 days mid-pilot.

## 2. Ideal pilot clinic profile (ICP)

**City:** Riyadh (founder is closest, in-person kickoff is possible, same time zone, same dialect). Secondary: Jeddah, Khobar.

**Specialty:** Dental, specifically general + cosmetic dentistry. Reason: repeat visit cycle is 6–12 months (perfect for reactivation), average ticket is 500–3,000 SAR (recovered revenue is meaningful), patient-to-clinic relationship is personal (messages from clinic feel natural, not corporate).

**Size band:**

- 2–4 chairs.
- 1,000–2,500 active patient records.
- 60–150 visits per month.
- 1–3 dentists, 1–3 receptionists.

Why this band: big enough that 300 dormant patients is a real cohort, small enough that the owner is personally involved in operations (he/she will pick up the phone, give us a clean list, and care about results).

**Patient base minimum:** 800 patients with a phone number and at least one prior visit in the last 24 months. Below this, the dormant cohort is too small to produce a publishable result.

**Tech-readiness signals (green flags):**

- Uses Excel or a basic PMS (not pen and paper).
- Already uses WhatsApp Business (not just personal WhatsApp).
- Owner replies to WhatsApp within a few hours, not days.
- Has tried at least one "tool" before (Snapchat ads, an old CRM, a booking widget) — meaning they're not allergic to trying things, but also have battle scars we can speak to.
- Has a working clinic-branded WhatsApp number that's been live > 6 months (Meta won't approve a fresh number fast enough for a 30-day pilot).

**Decision-maker access requirements:**

- Owner is the dentist (not an absentee investor).
- Owner answers his/her own phone.
- Owner can commit to a 30-minute kickoff call AND a 15-minute weekly check-in for 4 weeks.
- Owner can authorize a CSV export and a written agreement without going to a board.

**Anti-signals (clinics to AVOID for pilot):**

- Multi-branch chain with corporate marketing team. Slow approvals, watered-down case study, and they'll demand custom features.
- Clinic that has never used WhatsApp Business. We'd spend the whole pilot fighting Meta approvals.
- Brand-new clinic (< 12 months). No dormant patient base = no story to tell.
- Owner who immediately asks "how cheap can you make it" before hearing the value. Wrong relationship pattern for a pilot.
- Owner who wants to "test it on 50 patients first." Cohort too small to produce a defensible number.
- Clinic with a known reputation problem (negative Google reviews about staff conduct). Reactivation messages will land on already-angry patients and the pilot will look like a failure of Aooda, not of the clinic.

## 3. Success metrics (what we publish on landing page after)

**Primary headline metric (one number — this is what goes on the landing page hero):**

> **عدد المرضى اللي رجعوا للعيادة خلال 30 يوم** — Number of dormant patients who booked AND attended a visit within the 30-day pilot window, attributable to an Aooda WhatsApp message.

We do not publish revenue numbers as the headline. Two reasons: (a) revenue is sensitive for the clinic and they'll redact it from the case study, (b) Abu-Khalid trusts "12 مريض رجعوا" more than "47,000 ريال" — patients-returned is the unit he thinks in.

**Realistic target:** 8–25 returned patients out of a 300-patient dormant cohort (2.5%–8% return rate). Anything ≥ 10 is a publishable case study. We do NOT promise this number to the clinic — we only publish it after the fact.

**Supporting metrics (3 max, all publishable, all defensible to Abu-Khalid):**

1. **Reply rate to the first message** — % of patients who responded at all (positive or negative). Demonstrates the messages feel human, not spam. Target: > 25%.
2. **Time-to-first-return** — number of days from first send wave to the first patient walking back into the clinic. Proves speed-to-value. Target: < 7 days.
3. **Opt-out rate** — % who replied "إيقاف". This is the anti-vanity metric — we publish it specifically so Abu-Khalid can see we're honest. Target: < 5%. Publishing this number is what makes the case study trustworthy to the next clinic.

**Time-to-Value target (per the CS playbook):**

- **First reactivated patient walks into the clinic within 7 days** of Day 0. This is the "aha moment." If we hit this, the clinic stays bought-in for the remaining 23 days. If we miss it, we are in churn-risk territory and the founder personally calls the clinic owner.

**What we DON'T promise (write this on the landing page in small print):**

- We do not promise a specific number of returned patients.
- We do not promise specific revenue.
- We do not promise patients won't opt out.
- We do not promise the message will land in the inbox tab (WhatsApp delivery is Meta's domain, not ours).
- Results vary by clinic specialty, list quality, and seasonality.

This list of "don't promise" is itself a trust artifact — Abu-Khalid will respect a vendor who says what they can't do.

## 4. The 30-day timeline

**Day 0 — Kickoff (in-person at the clinic, 60 minutes)**

- Founder visits clinic. Signs the pilot agreement on the spot (pre-printed, two copies).
- Walk through the dashboard together on the receptionist's laptop.
- Agree on the WhatsApp number to use (must be the clinic's existing Business API number).
- Agree on the message tone with the owner — show 3 sample messages, get a verbal "نعم هذي تمشي."
- Set the weekly check-in time (same day, same time every week, 15 minutes, on WhatsApp voice call).
- Exchange direct phone numbers — owner has the founder's mobile, not a support email.

**Day 1–3 — Setup**

- Day 1: Clinic exports patient CSV (or receptionist does it with us on screen-share). We clean and de-dupe.
- Day 1: We identify the 300-patient dormant cohort, share it back with the owner for spot-check approval. Owner picks 10 names at random and verifies they're real dormant patients, not active ones.
- Day 2: Draft first-wave messages in Arabic Khaleeji. Owner reviews and edits. We do NOT send until owner says "روح."
- Day 3: Opt-in capture mechanism live — every existing patient was previously a clinic patient who consented to clinic contact, but we add the opt-out language to every message anyway (legal-drafter sign-off needed).

**Day 4–14 — First send wave + measurement**

- Day 4: First send wave — 100 patients (third of the cohort). We do NOT send all 300 at once; we want to see reply patterns first.
- Day 4–6: Monitor replies hourly. Founder watches the queue personally. Any "أوقف" gets removed within minutes. Any complaint (even mild) triggers an immediate WhatsApp call to the owner.
- Day 7: First TTV checkpoint — has at least one patient walked into the clinic? If yes, send confetti screenshot to owner. If no, founder calls owner, talks through what we're seeing, and decides whether to tune the message before wave 2.
- Day 7–8: Send wave 2 — next 100 patients, with any message tuning.
- Day 11: Send wave 3 — final 100 patients.
- Day 12–14: Follow-up message to non-responders from wave 1 only (one follow-up, never more).

**Day 15–30 — Optimization + write-up**

- Day 15: Mid-pilot review with owner. Show numbers so far. Ask: "Is anything in the clinic broken because of this? Are patients commenting in person?"
- Day 15–21: Iterate on message timing (morning vs afternoon test), tone (warmer vs more direct).
- Day 22–28: Final follow-ups to anyone who replied positively but hasn't booked yet — handed off to the clinic's receptionist with a script.
- Day 28: Lock metrics. Pull final numbers.
- Day 29: Draft the case study in Arabic (1-page PDF). Send to owner for approval.

**Day 30 — Case-study delivery + landing page publish**

- Owner approves the case study (or redacts what they want hidden).
- Founder visits clinic again, hand-delivers the printed case study, the invoice (15% of recovered revenue, capped at 3,000 SAR), and a hand-written thank-you note.
- Within 48 hours of owner sign-off: case study goes live on landing page with clinic name, owner's headshot (if approved), and the three metrics.
- Day 30 + 1: Ask owner for a 60-second WhatsApp video testimonial — this is the gold standard social proof, more valuable than the written case study.

## 5. The clinic-side conversation script (Arabic Khaleeji)

> Use this when the founder calls a target clinic owner. Read it like a human, not a script. Pause where there's a dash. Don't barrel through objections — let them land.

### Opening (15 seconds)

> "السلام عليكم دكتور [اسم] — معك [اسم المؤسس] من شركة عَودة. اتصل عليك بسرعة، 5 دقايق فقط — متفرّغ؟"

If yes → continue. If no → "متى يناسبك أرجع اتصل؟ بس أبي 5 دقايق منك، شي يخص عيادتك بالذات."

### The hook (30 seconds)

> "دكتور، باختصار: إحنا في عَودة قبل الإطلاق الرسمي. سوّينا نظام يرجّع المرضى اللي اختفوا — اللي ما رجعوا للعيادة من 6 شهور لسنة ونص — عن طريق رسالة واتساب طبيعية من رقم العيادة نفسها، مش من رقم غريب.
>
> أنا اليوم ما أبيع لك شي. أبي أختار **عيادة وحدة** في الرياض نشتغل معها 30 يوم، بدون أي رسوم مقدّمة، ونوثّق النتيجة ونحطها في موقعنا — العيادة تاخذ المرضى، وإحنا ناخذ القصة. سمعت عن عيادتك من [مصدر]، وحبيت أكلمك أنت بالذات."

### The offer (60 seconds)

> "اللي تاخذه أنت:
> – نشتغل على قائمة مرضاك أنت — ما نضيف ناس من برّا. 300 مريض اللي ما رجعوا من فترة.
> – الرسائل تطلع من رقم عيادتك، أنت تشوفها قبل ما تنزل، وأنت تقرّ كل كلمة.
> – ما تدفع شي مقدماً. ولا حتى إعداد. **بس لما يرجع مريض فعلاً ويدفع، تعطينا 15% من اللي دفعه — والحد الأقصى 3,000 ريال للشهر كله.**
> – لو ما رجع ولا مريض، تدفع صفر. وين الخسارة عليك؟
> – أعطيك تقرير أسبوعي بصفحة وحدة، وفي آخر الشهر تقرير نهائي تقدر تستخدمه أنت لنفسك.
>
> اللي ناخذه إحنا:
> – بإذنك، نكتب اسم عيادتك في صفحتنا مع الأرقام النهائية.
> – لو ما ارتحت، نشيلها — بدون نقاش.
> – وأنا شخصياً متابع المشروع من أول يوم لآخر يوم. رقمي معاك مباشرة."

### Objection handlers — top 5

**اعتراض 1: "المرضى ما يحبون رسائل آلية — يحسّونها spam"**

> "صح كلامك دكتور — لو الرسالة آلية أو مكتوبة بنبرة دعاية، تضرّ. عشان كذا الرسالة عندنا تطلع من رقم عيادتك، ومكتوبة بلهجة طبيعية — أقرب لرسالة من موظفة الاستقبال منها لإعلان. وأهم شي: **أنت تقرّ كل رسالة قبل ما تنزل.** لو ما عجبتك، ما تنزل. وفي خانة 'للإيقاف رد بـ إيقاف' — أي مريض ما يبي يستقبل، يطلع برّا القائمة فوراً. الـ opt-out نحطه في التقرير الأسبوعي عشان تشوف بنفسك إنه أقل من 5%."

**اعتراض 2: "كيف الرسائل تبان من العيادة مش من شركة مجهولة؟"**

> "نشتغل على رقم واتساب الأعمال حق عيادتك مباشرة — مش رقمنا. المريض لما يستقبل الرسالة، يشوف اسم عيادتك واللوقو حقّها — نفس اللي يشوفه لو حجز موعد عادي. إحنا في الخلفية، المريض ما يعرف إنّا موجودين. هذا أهم شي عندنا، لأنه لو طلعت الرسالة من رقم غريب، ضاعت الثقة."

**اعتراض 3: "لو رسالة غلط أو محرجة، تضر أكثر من تنفع"**

> "هذا هاجس حقيقي وأنا معك فيه. عشان كذا أول شي نسويه: نعطيك 3 نماذج رسائل في اليوم الأول، وأنت تعدّل أو تشطب أو ترفض — وما نرسل ولا رسالة وحدة قبل ما تقول 'روح'. وفي أول موجة نرسل لـ 100 مريض فقط، مش 300 دفعة وحدة — لو شفنا أي رد فعل سلبي، نوقف ونعدّل قبل الموجة الثانية. هذا التزام مكتوب في الاتفاقية."

**اعتراض 4: "السعر كم؟ لو أغلى من موظف جزء وقت ما يستاهل"**

> "بصراحة دكتور: في الـ 30 يوم، أقصى شي ممكن تدفعه 3,000 ريال — وهذا فقط لو رجعوا لك مرضى وعطوك إيرادات حقيقية. لو ما رجع ولا مريض، تدفع صفر. موظفة جزء وقت تكلفك 3,000 إلى 4,000 ريال شهرياً، ضمان أو ما ضمان. الفرق: نحن ما ندفع لنا غير لما ترجع لك إيرادات فعلاً تشوفها في الصندوق."

**اعتراض 5: "بيانات المرضى آمنة؟ ما أبي أحد يشوف بياناتهم"**

> "صريح معك: البيانات مخزّنة على خوادم في المنطقة، ما تطلع. ولا موظف عندنا يفتح قاعدة المرضى — كل شي مؤتمت. وفي الاتفاقية بند صريح: لو وقفنا الباي لوت، نحذف كل البيانات خلال 48 ساعة ونرسل لك تأكيد كتابي. أنا أقدر أعطيك نسخة من الاتفاقية اليوم تقرأها مع المحامي حقّك قبل ما توقّع — ما في عجلة."

### The ask (close)

> "دكتور، أنا ما أبي قرار اليوم. أبي تعطيني فرصة آجي العيادة هالأسبوع — 30 دقيقة، أوريك النظام من غير وعود، ولو ما عجبك ما في إلزام. متى يناسبك؟ الثلاثا الصبح ولا الخميس بعد العصر؟"

(The "either/or" close is intentional — gives him agency over the time but not over the yes/no.)

## 6. Risks + mitigations

**What kills a pilot:**

| Risk                                                    | What kills it                                    | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bad patient list (wrong numbers, outdated)              | Low delivery rate → no returns → no case study   | Owner spot-checks 10 random names before send. We pull a fresh export, not last year's.                                                                                         |
| Owner stops engaging after Day 7                        | No feedback loop → wrong tone → opt-outs spike   | Weekly check-in is in the signed agreement. If owner misses two in a row, founder shows up at clinic in person.                                                                 |
| WhatsApp Business API approval delay                    | Can't send → pilot dead                          | ICP requires clinic to have existing Business API number, > 6 months old. We never run a pilot on a fresh approval.                                                             |
| Negative patient reaction (even one complaint)          | Owner loses confidence, even if numbers are good | First wave is 100 patients, not 300. Founder watches replies hourly Day 4–6. Any complaint → immediate call to owner same hour.                                                 |
| Pilot succeeds but owner refuses case study publication | We did the work, no publishable result           | Pilot agreement includes pre-committed permission to publish anonymized metrics (no clinic name) if owner declines named publication. Named publication is a separate sign-off. |

**What we do if zero patients return in 14 days:**

1. Founder calls owner same day, before the owner calls us. Own the bad news.
2. Diagnose together: list quality? message tone? timing? seasonality (Ramadan, Hajj, summer travel)?
3. Offer one of: (a) extend pilot by 14 days at no charge, (b) restart with a different cohort, (c) end clean with zero charged and full data export within 48 hours.
4. Internal: log the failure mode in a "what we learned" doc. This is the most valuable thing we can take from a failed pilot.
5. We do NOT spin the result. If we publish anything about this pilot, it's anonymized and honest about what didn't work.

**What we do if a patient complains:**

1. Receptionist or owner forwards the complaint to founder within 1 hour (set up the channel on Day 0).
2. Founder writes a personal apology message in the owner's voice, sends to owner for approval, owner sends from the clinic number. We do not send anything to a complaining patient ourselves.
3. Patient is removed from all future sends AND we offer the owner the option to credit the patient's next visit (clinic's discretion, our expense up to 200 SAR).
4. We add a note to the case study: "X complaints received, all resolved within 24 hours." Hiding this would be worse than disclosing it.
5. If complaints exceed 3 in any 7-day window, founder pauses all sending and calls owner to decide whether to continue.
